Somewhere in most people's idea of a synthesiser there is a button that makes it
sound good. A preset, a knob marked _warmth_, a thing the people who can do this
know about and you do not.

There isn't one. There is a shelf of parts, and knowing what each of them does.

## The three shelves, one last time

Every patch in this tutorial, and every patch you will ever build, is made of the
same three kinds of thing.

- A **source** makes a sound out of nothing: an oscillator, a noise generator, a
  sample. It is the only thing here that is loud on its own.
- A **modifier** changes a sound that already exists: a filter, an amplifier, a
  delay. Hand it silence and it gives you silence back.
- A **controller** makes no sound at all. An envelope, an LFO, a keyboard. It
  moves something else, and what it moves is a parameter of a source or of a
  modifier.

That is the whole taxonomy, and the reason it is worth memorising is what follows
from it: **a synthesiser is not its modules, it is the way they are patched.**
The same oscillator, filter and envelope are a bass, a string section and a
thunderclap depending only on what is connected to what and how fast the
controllers move. You have now built all three out of one voice.

## What you have

A vocabulary, mostly. _Attack_ is a length of time, not a feeling. _Resonance_ is
a peak at the cutoff frequency, and it is why a filter sweep whistles. _Tremolo_
is on the amplifier and _vibrato_ is on the pitch, and confusing them is the
oldest mistake in the manual.

And a habit: when a sound is wrong, ask which of the three shelves is wrong
before you touch anything. Too dull is a modifier. Too flat is a controller. Too
thin is usually a source.

## Where to go next

Make something. The [Playground](/learn/playground) is the whole voice with a
link you can send; every sound in the gallery started as a paragraph in
[Synth Secrets](https://www.soundonsound.com/series/synth-secrets) and ended as
thirty-one numbers.

Then build it yourself, in your own page. The
[documentation](/docs/quick-start) is the same modules this tutorial is made of,
one page each, every one of them with a running example — and the
[guide](/docs/guide) is the half this tutorial did not cover: scheduling,
offline rendering, and what to do when the audio thread is late.

The library is on [GitHub](https://github.com/danigb/synthlet), and it is
small enough to read. If what you want is to play notes rather than to build the
instrument that plays them, [smplr](https://github.com/danigb/smplr) is the
sibling project: sampled instruments, the same audio context, no synthesis
required.
