---
title: Amplifiers
description: "The most overlooked module in a synthesiser: a gain with a signal on it. Where it sits decides whether it is making a sound or shaping one."
---

An amplifier is a thing that changes how big a signal is. That is the entire
specification, and it sounds too simple to spend a chapter on — until you notice
that the envelope in the last chapter did nothing on its own. Something had to
take that contour and use it, and that something is here.

Four pages. The first is the amplifier in the audio path with an oscillator
wired to its control input, which is tremolo and is also the plainest possible
demonstration of what "voltage controlled" means. The second is the same module
standing in a control cable instead, where it is called "envelope amount" and
where most synthesists meet it every day without ever noticing what it is. The
third is the slider that adds a steady offset to the control signal and makes
the synth refuse to stop. The fourth is what happens when you ask an amplifier
for more than it can give.

Reid spends Part 9 arguing that the distinction he opened with — amplifiers in
the audio chain versus amplifiers in the control chain — is not a distinction at
all. This chapter is that argument, one widget at a time.
