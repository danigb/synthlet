An envelope generator has no volume control. Whatever its four sliders say, its
output runs from nothing to full scale and stops there, which is fine when it is
driving the loudness of a note and useless when it is driving something with
units. Two thousand hertz of filter sweep and six thousand are different sounds,
and the envelope cannot tell you which you are getting.

So something has to stand between the envelope and its destination and decide
how much of it arrives. That something is an amplifier — the same module as the
last page, in a cable nobody listens to.

<Patch id="amplifiers/env-amount" show={["amount", "cutoff"]} />

<Try>

With **Envelope amount** at zero, play a few notes. The filter never moves: a
dull constant timbre with an envelope on its loudness and nothing else. The
contour generator is running the whole time, and none of it is getting through.

</Try>

<Try>

Bring **Envelope amount** up to a couple of thousand and play again. Every note
now opens the filter and lets it close, and the harmonics near the left of the
spectrum light up and go out with each key. The envelope has not changed shape.
It is being scaled on the way.

</Try>

<Try>

Set **Cutoff** somewhere in the middle and drop **Envelope amount** to match.
**Cutoff** is where the sweep starts and **Envelope amount** is how far above
that it reaches — the two ends of the same journey, which is why every synth
that has one has the other next to it.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

Look at the diagram. The box marked Param is drawn as a modifier, on the same
shelf as the filter, and it is in a cable that never reaches your ears. That is
deliberate: the majority of the amplifiers inside an analogue synthesiser are
not in the audio path at all, they are attenuating control voltages, and they
are the same circuit.

It follows that a module has no fixed shelf. A gain is a modifier when a
sawtooth passes through it, and a modifier still when an envelope does; whether
anyone can hear the result is a question about where its output is plugged, not
about the module. Reid spends Part 9 on that argument, and this knob is the
version of it you use every day.

</Aside>
