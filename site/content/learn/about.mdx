Learning Synthlet is not a new idea. It is two old ones put together, with a
third thing neither of them could do.

## Synth Secrets

Gordon Reid wrote [Synth Secrets](https://www.soundonsound.com/series/synth-secrets)
in Sound On Sound between 1999 and 2004: sixty-three monthly parts that start
with a sine wave and end with a Hammond organ, and that explain synthesis as
physics you can hear rather than a list of knobs. It is still the best thing
written on the subject, and it is where this tutorial's structure comes from —
sound, then the things that shape it, then the things that move the shapers.

Reid's articles have one limitation, and it is the one he could do nothing
about: a printed magazine cannot make a sound. Every figure is a picture of a
waveform you have to imagine hearing.

## Learning Synths

Ableton's [Learning Synths](https://learningsynths.ableton.com/) solved exactly
that. It is an interactive introduction that puts one playable synth in front of
you and reveals its controls a few at a time, so that every claim is something
you do rather than something you read. Its pace, and its insistence that a
lesson is one idea with one knob, is where this tutorial's shape comes from.

Learning Synths does not show you its code, because its code is not the point.

## And the third thing

Here every lesson ends with the patch it is made of — the actual
[synthlet](https://github.com/danigb/synthlet) modules and the actual
`connect()` calls, copy-pasteable into your own page. The code shown is the code
that ran. That is the part neither of the other two could offer, and it costs
nothing, because the examples already are that code.

## Provenance

Synth Secrets is Sound On Sound's copyrighted text and Learning Synths is
Ableton's. Neither is reproduced here.

What happens instead: the ideas are paraphrased in this tutorial's own words and
cited to the part they came from, so that you can go and read the original.
Where a sentence of Reid's is worth quoting it is quoted short, in quotation
marks, attributed, and never in place of an explanation. Nothing here is a
translation, a transcription or a re-hosting of either work.

This is the same rule the library holds for the instruments it models: inspired
by, not cloned from.
