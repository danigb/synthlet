Part 3 draws three contours side by side and invites you to name the
instruments from the shapes alone: a rectangle, a lump that falls back to a
ledge, and a spike with a long tail. They are an organ, a trombone and a
thunderclap. This page loads those three and a flute into a second synthesiser
beside yours, played from the same keys.

<Patch
  id="envelopes/matching"
  show={["target", "compare", "attack", "decay", "sustain", "release"]}
/>

**Hear the target** points the keys at the target instead of at your sound.
Everything else about the two is identical, so anything you can hear between
them is the envelope and only the envelope.

<Try>

Start on **Organ** and flip the switch back and forth. There is nothing to do:
your sliders already match it. On, full level, off — the contour is very nearly
a rectangle, and it is so much an organ's that the shape carries the name even
in patches that sound nothing like one.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch to **Trombone** and go hunting. It speaks slowly, reaches its loudest at
the end of the attack and falls back to a ledge a little below that, so the
attack wants around a tenth of a second and the sustain wants to be high but not
maximum. Then **Thunderclap**: everything at the front, no sustain at all, and a
decay you measure in seconds rather than in tenths.

</Try>

<Try>

Last, **Flute** — which you can match in about four seconds, and then improve
on. Its release is five hundredths of a second, so every note is cut off rather
than allowed to leave, and a line played quickly sounds like the player is
swallowing. Take your own **Release** up to about a fifth of a second: the notes
join up, and you no longer match the target.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

That last mismatch is worth more than the match. Reid hits it in Part 54 while
building a flute: the contour is right on paper, and a line played with it
sounds as though the player keeps sucking air in. The cause is the device rather
than the setting — a contour that must begin at zero digs a hole under whatever
was sounding a moment ago.

Lengthening the release papers over the hole, at the cost of the shape you were
copying. The next page has the other half of the explanation.

</Aside>
