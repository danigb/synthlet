Four stages get you a very long way, and then they stop. Reid's example is a
brass section played hard: the note goes "psst" at the front, drops back to
something quieter and duller, and only then swells to its full size. Write that
down as an ADSR and you cannot, because an ADSR is always loudest at the end of
its attack and its sustain always begins wherever the decay left off.

The answer is older than the problem. Take two simple attack-and-decay contours,
add them together, and send the sum to one destination: a fast one for the spit
and a slow one for the swell.

<Patch id="envelopes/beyond" show={["spit", "swell", "vibratoDelay"]} />

<Try>

Hold a note and watch the contour. Then pull **Spit** to zero and hold another.
The front of the note goes flat and what is left is the swell arriving on its
own — the same note, minus its consonant.

</Try>

<Try>

Put **Spit** back and take **Swell** from fast to slow. This is the slow
contour's attack, so it decides how late the second half of the note turns up.
At the slow end the note is still getting louder two seconds after you played
it, which is a shape the last four pages could not make at any setting.

</Try>

<Try>

Hold a long note and move **Vibrato delay**. At zero the wobble is there from
the start and sounds like a machine. Around a second it arrives after the note
has established itself, which is what a player does, and what the delay control
on an LFO is for.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

It is worth being precise about which limitation is doing the damage, because
most of the nine an ADSR has are harmless. The fatal pair are that the peak is
always at the end of the attack, and that the sustain always starts where the
decay finished. The spit brass violates both at once: its loudest moment is at
the front, and the level it ends up holding is above the level it fell to.

Two envelopes summed get around both because addition has no opinion about
order. A five-stage envelope generator with editable break points would do the
same job in one module, and this library does not have one yet — so the CV
mixer, which is what an audio-rate parameter accepting several connections
really is, remains the honest answer.

</Aside>
