Pressing a key on a synthesiser sends a pitch and two separate timing signals.
The <Term>gate</Term> goes high on the press and stays high for as long as the
key is down, so it is the signal that knows when you let go. A pulse at the
moment of the press, and nothing more, is a <Term>trigger</Term>. A contour
generator needs both: the trigger says start, and the gate says keep going until
I say otherwise. The library's own account of the contract is in
[gates and triggers](/docs/gates-and-triggers).

This widget is one voice, so its notes cannot overlap. What it does when you
play as though they could is the entire lesson.

<Patch id="envelopes/gates" show={["legato", "attack"]} />

<Try>

With **Legato** off, play a run with the letter keys and hold each one a little
too long, so that the next note starts before you have let the last one go.
Every note speaks: the gate closes and reopens under your fingers, so every note
gets its own attack.

</Try>

<Try>

Turn **Legato** on and play the same sloppy run. Only the first note is
articulated. The gate never falls — something is always held — so the contour
never restarts, and the notes after the first are pitch changes inside one long
note.

</Try>

<Try>

Take **Attack** up to a third of a second and play the run both ways. Off, every
note swells from nothing and the line stutters. On, the line swells once and
then simply moves about. Neither is wrong; they are two different instruments,
and you would choose between them by what you were playing.

</Try>

<Aside kind="history">

Reid worked this out with a Minimoog and an ARP Odyssey side by side in a live
rig. He preferred playing fast solos on the Odyssey and could not say why, and
assumed it was the keyboards. It was not. The Odyssey sends a trigger and
retriggers its contours on every key press whether or not the previous note has
been released; the Minimoog sends only a gate, so an overlapping run loses its
punch after the first note. The toggle above is exactly that difference, with
nothing else changed.

</Aside>
