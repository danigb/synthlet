---
title: Envelopes
description: How a sound begins, how it holds, and how it leaves — the four numbers that turn a tone into a note, and the two places they are not enough.
---

Chapter 1 left you with a tone that starts when you press Play and stops when
you press it again. Nothing in the world behaves like that. A note is an event:
it arrives at some speed, it settles at some level, and it takes some time to
go, and those three facts are most of what tells you whether you are listening
to an organ, a trombone or a thunderclap.

The device that describes them is the envelope generator, and the first three
pages of this chapter are one synthesiser gaining one of its sliders at a time —
the same widget, three times, so that each slider arrives on its own and you can
hear exactly what it does. The fourth page is an exercise: four target contours
from the book, and four sliders to hit them with. The fifth is about the signal
that starts the envelope rather than the envelope itself, which turns out to
decide how a fast line feels under the hands. The last one is the page where a
four-stage envelope runs out of road, and two envelopes take over.
