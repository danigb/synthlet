The same synthesiser, two more sliders. **Decay** is a time and **Sustain** is a
level, and that asymmetry is the thing worth getting straight early: of the four
stages an envelope generator has, three are durations and this one is a height.

<Patch id="envelopes/adsr" show={["attack", "decay", "sustain"]} />

<Try>

Put **Sustain** at the top and sweep **Decay** from end to end while a key is
down. Nothing happens. Decay is the fall from the peak of the attack to the
sustain level, and when those two are the same there is nowhere to fall — the
slider is connected and has no work to do.

</Try>

<Try>

Bring **Sustain** to zero and set **Decay** to about a third of a second. Hold
the key. The note dies anyway, with your finger still on it. That is a plucked
string, and no amount of holding will make a guitar ring for ever.

</Try>

<Try>

Now **Sustain** to about a third of the way up, and move **Decay** slowly from
fast to slow. The note keeps arriving at the same place; only the journey
changes. Fast is a percussive front with a body behind it, which is most
electric pianos. Slow is a swell that never quite settles down.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

"Sustain is a level" is the most commonly misread of the four letters, because
in ordinary English sustain is a duration. Instruments are what make the
synthesiser's reading the right one. A plucked or struck string is given all its
energy in one event and then leaks it away: its sustain level is zero, and its
decay is the entire sound. A bowed, blown or electrically driven sound has
someone feeding it continuously, so it can hold a level for as long as the
feeding goes on — and what decides how long that is turns out not to be the
envelope generator at all. It is your finger on the key, which is the next page.

</Aside>
