---
title: Sound
description: What a sound is made of, the three things you can change about one, and the four raw materials a synthesiser starts from.
---

Before a synthesiser can shape a sound it has to make one, and before either of
those is worth doing it helps to know what a sound is. This chapter answers that
in one sentence — a sound is a stack of sine waves, and which ones are in the
stack is what you hear as its character — and then spends four pages making that
sentence audible. You will build a sawtooth one harmonic at a time and take it
apart again, separate pitch from loudness from timbre by moving three knobs that
each move exactly one of them, learn to recognise the four waveforms by ear, and
meet the one source that has no pitch at all.

The fifth page has no sound in it. It is the map: the whole catalogue of modules
sorted onto three shelves, which is the arrangement every chapter after this one
assumes. Read it last, and come back to it whenever a module you meet does not
seem to belong anywhere.
