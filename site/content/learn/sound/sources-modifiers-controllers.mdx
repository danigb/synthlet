This page makes no sound. It is the map, and it comes at the end of the first
chapter because everything after it is a module with a name, and thirty names are
easier to hold on to as three piles than as a list.

<Map />

A <Term>source</Term> makes a signal out of nothing. Those are the oscillators —
[PolyblepOscillator](/docs/polyblep) and [WavetableOscillator](/docs/wavetable) —
plus [Noise](/docs/noise), which you met on the last page, and the odd ones like
[Karplus-Strong](/docs/ks) that make a whole plucked string in one box.

A <Term>modifier</Term> takes a signal in and puts a changed one out. The filter
you have been using, [Svf](/docs/state-variable-filter), is one; so is any
amplifier, including [AdsrAmp](/docs/adsr-amp); so are the distortions like
[ClipAmp](/docs/clip-amp) and the delays like
[DigitalDelay](/docs/digital-delay).

A <Term>controller</Term> makes a signal nobody is meant to hear. It exists to be
pointed at something else's knob: [AdsrEnv](/docs/adsr) for a shape that happens
once per note, [Lfo](/docs/lfo) for one that repeats, [Param](/docs/param) for
arithmetic on the way, [Clock](/docs/clock) for a steady tick.

## The shelves are not fixed

Here is the part that makes the arrangement worth learning rather than
memorising. Nothing about a module decides which shelf it sits on. Slow an
oscillator to 5 Hz, point it at a filter's cutoff, and it is a controller — it is
producing vibrato and nobody would call it a source. Speed an LFO up to 500 Hz,
send it to the output, and it is a source; you can hear it.

The same is true of a filter with enough resonance to oscillate on its own, and
of an envelope fast enough to be a click. It is not where a signal comes from
that matters but where it is going <Book part={3} />, and that is why the two
arrows in the drawing run down the outside rather than through the middle.

Reid put this at the very end of Synth Secrets, after sixty-two parts of
particulars. It is at the front here because it is cheaper to learn first.
