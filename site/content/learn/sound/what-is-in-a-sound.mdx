---
title: What is in a sound
description: Every sound you can hear is a stack of sine waves. Building a sawtooth out of them, and taking it apart again.
---

A sound is air pressure changing over time, and any change you can hear can be
written as a stack of sine waves — one for the pitch you hear, and a series of
quieter ones above it. Those are the harmonics, and their pattern is the whole
of what we call timbre.

A sawtooth wave is the honest case: every harmonic present, the nth one at 1/n
of the fundamental's amplitude. Strip the upper ones away and it dulls until it
is a sine again. That is the entire idea behind a filter, arrived at before the
word has been used.
