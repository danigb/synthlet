Everything so far has put the delay at the end, where it adds ambience. This
page moves it to the front. A violin string vibrating at the bridge is close to
a sawtooth, and a violin does not sound like a sawtooth; the difference is a
hollow wooden box with resonances of its own. A delay short enough to fit inside
a cycle of the note has resonances of its own too.

<Patch id="effects/body" show={["delay", "feedback", "cutoff"]} />

<Try>

Play a low note with **Delay** at 5 ms. The marks on the spectrum are at every
multiple of one over the delay, and the note's harmonics are emphasised where
they land near one and suppressed where they do not. Now play up the keyboard:
the marks stay exactly where they are. A body does not move with the note, which
is why the character of each note is different.

</Try>

<Try>

Hold a note and sweep **Delay** from 20 ms down to 1. At the top it is an audible
flutter; somewhere around 10 it stops being a repeat and becomes a pitch; by 2 or
3 it is a small hard box. Reid puts the useful range for an instrument body at
about 1 to 4 ms, and works it out from the speed of sound: a cavity with its
lowest resonance at 500 Hz is about a third of a metre long.

</Try>

<Try>

Turn **Feedback** down to zero and back up to 0.9. That knob is how long the box
rings, which is the same quantity as a room's reverberation time measured on a
much smaller room. **Cutoff** is the ordinary filter, still there, still after
the body rather than instead of it.

</Try>

<Aside kind="history">

This is one of the few Synth Secrets patches you could not try on a modern
workstation, because its effects are bolted to the end of the chain. Reid's
readers needed a modular, or an ARP 2600 or EMS VCS3, whose spring reverb could
be patched anywhere. A spring is far too long for a violin — it would model a
cavity four metres deep — so he reaches for the bucket brigade instead, which
is the only thing in 1969 that could be made short enough.

</Aside>
