---
title: "Effects"
description: "Five effects that are all the same delay line with a different number in it, ending with the one that stops being an effect at all."
---

Synth Secrets ends on delay lines. Reid spends his last four instalments on
them, and the reason is not that he ran out of oscillators: it is that a delay
line turns out to be the cheapest general-purpose thing in the whole studio.
Store a slice of a signal, hold it, hand it on at a rate a clock decides, and
the length of time you hold it for decides what the device is called.

Hold it for a second and it is an echo. Hold it for a second and feed some of
the output back to the input and the echo becomes a row of echoes dying away.
Chain three of them at times that do not divide into one another and the repeats
stop being countable and become a room. Hold it for twenty milliseconds and
wobble the clock and three copies of one signal sound like three players.
Hold it for five and the copy arrives inside the cycle of the note itself,
where it stops being a copy and becomes a resonance.

That is this chapter, in that order. Every page is the same module with a
different number in it, and the number is the whole difference. The last page is
the one Reid is most pleased about: take that very short delay and put it
_before_ the filter rather than after the amplifier, and the patch stops
sounding like a synth with an effect on it and starts sounding like an
instrument with a body. In 2001 he called that physical modelling and pointed
out that you did not need a DSP to do it — you needed a modular synth, a
bucket-brigade device, and somebody willing to patch the reverb into the wrong
place.
