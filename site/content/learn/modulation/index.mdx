---
title: Modulation
description: One signal moving another signal's parameter, at every speed from a slow wobble to a new timbre.
---

Everything so far has been set and then left alone. You chose a waveform, dialled
in a cutoff, shaped an envelope and fired it. Modulation is the moment a control
stops being a number and becomes a signal: something else inside the synth moves
it while the note is sounding, and that movement is most of what separates an
instrument you want to listen to from a beep with a shape on it.

The chapter is one idea at eight speeds. It opens with an oscillator running far
below hearing, plugged into three different sockets — which gives vibrato,
tremolo and growl, three effects that every book treats as three things and that
turn out to be one oscillator and a switch. Two pages later that oscillator is
moving the width of a pulse rather than its pitch, and one page after that it has
been replaced by a random voltage that changes once a beat.

Then the speed goes up. Somewhere above twenty hertz a wobble stops being a
wobble: you no longer hear something moving, you hear a different tone. The same
three wires, driven at audio frequency, become amplitude modulation, ring
modulation and FM — and the last two pages arrive at timbre from the other end
entirely, with nine sine waves you mix by hand and one oscillator resetting
another.
