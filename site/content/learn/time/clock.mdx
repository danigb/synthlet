Everything in the first five chapters waited for you. A key, a slider, a gate
button: you did something and the synthesiser answered. This chapter is about
the other half of a modular synthesiser — the half that does things on its own —
and all of it starts with a clock.

A clock is not a timer. It is a module with an output, and what comes out is a
signal like any other: it can be scaled, offset, divided, and sent anywhere a
parameter will take a cable. It lives among the samples rather than among the
callbacks, which is why the widget below keeps time while the page around it is
busy.

<Patch id="time/clock" show={["bpm", "pulseWidth", "beatsPerBar"]} />

<Try>

Press Play. Two voices: a high tick on every beat and a low one at the top of
every bar. Move **Tempo** while it runs — the change lands on the next beat and
nothing stumbles, because the tempo is a parameter and not a `setInterval`.

</Try>

<Try>

Widen **Pulse width** and the ticks lengthen into notes. The scope is looking at
the gate rather than at the sound, so what you are watching is the control
signal the amplifiers read: a rectangle that is high for that fraction of each
beat.

</Try>

<Try>

Take **Beats per bar** from four to three and the low voice arrives a beat
earlier each time round. At zero it stops: there is no bar, only beats.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

The clock has two kinds of output and they are not interchangeable. Its own
output is a <Term>phase</Term> — a ramp rising from zero to one across each beat
— and a phase is what a rhythm generator needs, because subdividing a clock means
multiplying its ramp. The gate is a pulse, high for the pulse width, and a pulse
is what an envelope needs. The bar pair exists for the same reason and cannot be
recovered downstream: the ramp during beat one is identical to the ramp during
beat three, so the count has to belong to whoever owns the phase.

</Aside>
