A low-pass filter keeps what is below the corner. Swap two components around and
it keeps what is above instead. Put those two in series and only a band in the
middle survives; run them side by side and everything survives except that band.
Four responses, one idea — and in this widget one module, because a state
variable filter computes all of them at once and a switch decides which output
you hear.

<Try>

Press Play with **Type** on **Low-pass**, then walk down the list. **High-pass**
is the same sawtooth with its body removed and only the fizz left. **Band-pass**
keeps a slice around the mark and throws away both ends. **Notch** is its
opposite: everything except that slice, which is why it barely changes the sound
at all until you move the cutoff.

</Try>

<Try>

Leave it on **Band-pass** and sweep **Cutoff** up and down. The same knob means
something different under every setting of the switch: the edge of what is kept,
the edge of what is removed, or the centre of the slice. The mark on the
spectrum always sits at the number, and what happens around it is the switch's
business.

</Try>

<Try>

Turn **Resonance** up to 10 and sweep the cutoff again. On the low-pass it is a
peak riding the corner; on the notch it makes the hole narrower and deeper; on
the band-pass it turns a slice into something close to a single tone. This is

<Term>Q</Term>, and the next lesson is about what happens when you keep turning
it.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

Series and parallel are how these responses were built before anyone integrated
them. A low-pass and a high-pass in series is a band-pass, provided their corners
are separated — with both at the same frequency the signal is attenuated
everywhere, by 6 dB at its loudest point, which is unusable. The same two in
parallel, low-pass at 1 kHz and high-pass at 5 kHz, recombine into a notch. Reid
is careful to add that recombining them is where the trouble starts: each path
phase-shifts what it passes, and the sum of two differently shifted signals is
the comb from the first page of this chapter.

</Aside>
