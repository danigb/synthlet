Reid opens his filter chapter without a filter, and so does this one. Two
identical signals offset in time do not simply get louder when you add them
together: at some frequencies they reinforce, at others they cancel outright,
and which is which depends only on how far apart in time they are.

<Patch id="filters/comb" show={["delay", "mix", "waveform"]} />

<Try>

Leave **Waveform** on **Sine 100 Hz** and **Delay** at 5 ms, and press Play. It
is almost silent. Now switch to **Sine 200 Hz** without touching anything else
and it comes roaring back, louder than one oscillator alone. Five milliseconds
is half a cycle of 100 Hz and a whole cycle of 200 Hz, and half a cycle apart is
the definition of cancelling.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch to **Sawtooth 100 Hz**, which contains both of those frequencies and every
one above them, and drag **Delay** slowly from 10 ms down towards zero. The
vertical marks are the predicted cancellations, and the holes in the trace walk
up the spectrum alongside them as the delay shrinks. This shape is why the
arrangement is called a <Term>comb filter</Term>. Swept rather than set, it is
also a flanger.

</Try>

<Try>

Put the delay back to 5 ms on the 100 Hz sine and pull **Mix** down. The silence
fills back in long before the copy is gone. Two things add up to nothing only
when they are exactly the same size, so total cancellation lives at one setting
and nowhere else.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

A delay of t seconds turns every frequency into a phase shift, and the shift is
different for each one: a frequency whose period fits an odd number of
half-cycles into t arrives inverted and cancels, one that fits a whole number
arrives in step and doubles. That puts the notches at odd multiples of 1/2t,
which is the even spacing you can see. Reid's own result is the surprising part:
adding a sawtooth to a copy of itself half a cycle late does not produce silence,
it produces a sawtooth an octave up, because the harmonics that survive are the
even ones. Combining out-of-phase signals almost never cancels everything.

</Aside>
