Every filter so far has stood still, which makes it a tone control: powerful,
but something you set rather than something you play. The missing piece is that
the cutoff can be a voltage instead of a knob, and once it is, anything that
makes a voltage can move it — a contour once per key, an oscillator too slow to
hear, or both.

<Try>

Play a note. The filter opens and shuts on every key press: that is the
envelope. Pull **Envelope amount** down to zero and play again — the sound is
still there and it is dull and dead, because the cutoff is now parked at the
floor and nothing moves it. The envelope is 0 to 1 whatever its times say; this
knob is what decides how many hertz that is worth.

</Try>

<Try>

Put the amount back and take **Decay** from 0.02 up to 2 seconds. Short is a
click and a thud; around 0.3 is the plucked bass that carried most of the
records made between 1972 and 1985; two seconds is a slow opening that outlasts
most notes. Nothing about the pitch or the loudness changed.

</Try>

<Try>

Hold a note and bring **LFO depth** up, then move **LFO rate** through its three
zones. Near 0.1 Hz you get a tide, useful for something that sits under a track
for a minute. Around 1 or 2 Hz it is a wah. Push it to 15 Hz and it becomes a
growl, which is one of the standard ways of faking a brass instrument.

</Try>

<Try>

Turn **Resonance** up and play the envelope again. The peak marks where the
corner is at each instant, so the more resonance there is, the more of the
movement you hear.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

Both modulators arrive at the same parameter and are summed with the number
already in it, so the cutoff at any instant is a floor plus a contour plus a
wobble. That is worth knowing because it is how every modulation matrix works:
destinations add. It is also why the envelope reaches the filter through a box
of its own. A contour generator's output is the same 0 to 1 whatever its knobs
say, so something has to decide how much of it arrives: Reid draws an amplifier
standing in the control cable, and that amplifier is the envelope amount knob.

</Aside>
