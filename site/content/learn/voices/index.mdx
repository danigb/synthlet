---
title: "Notes and voices"
description: "What happens between a key going down and a sound coming out: which note wins, how many can sound at once, which one dies, and what a preset really is."
---

A keyboard is a lie that every synthesiser has to maintain. Ten fingers arrive
in whatever order they like, and underneath there is a fixed amount of circuitry
that has to decide what to do about them. This chapter is the four decisions.

The first is the one a monosynth cannot avoid. One voice, four keys held: which
note sounds? Parts 18 gives four answers, all of them shipped in real
instruments, and the choice changes what the instrument is to play rather than
how it sounds. The second is where the filter and the envelope sit. Four
oscillators through one filter and one envelope is a **paraphonic** instrument,
and it behaves in a way nobody expects the first time they meet it: the second
note you add has no attack of its own, because the envelope is already open.

The third is what happens when the voices run out, which is a question with no
correct answer and four defensible ones. The fourth is the file format all of
this ends up in — a preset, which turns out to be a flat object of parameter
values plus the two or three options that belong to a sound rather than to an
instrument.

**Duophony, which is Part 19, is skipped.** A duophonic keyboard splits two held
notes between two oscillators inside one otherwise monophonic voice, and it
exists because 1974 could not afford a second filter. Nothing in this library
models it, and nothing should: a duophonic instrument is a polyphonic instrument
with a deliberate hole in it, and the interesting half of the idea — sharing a
filter between voices — is the paraphonic page instead.
