---
title: "Recipes"
description: "The second half of Synth Secrets is a parameter table per instrument. This is that half, with every table turned into something you can press."
---

From Part 23 onwards Gordon Reid stops explaining synthesis and starts using
it. Brass, strings, flutes, pianos, organs, five drums and a Leslie cabinet:
thirty-odd articles that are, structurally, all the same article. He measures a
real instrument, works out which of its features a subtractive synthesiser can
reach, builds the patch on a named machine — a Minimoog, an ARP Axxe, a Korg
MS-20, a Roland JX10 — and prints the settings.

That is the most useful and the most frustrating thing in the series. The
numbers are real, and where he could not measure one he says so — but a table
of knob positions for a synthesiser you do not own is a table you cannot check.
So each of the fourteen pages here is a recipe you can hear, with the book's own
numbers on the controls and a mark on the spectrum wherever Reid made a claim
about where a frequency should land.

**Nothing here is on the core path**: the chapters up to Modulation teach the
instrument, and this one uses it. Where Reid gives a number the lesson uses it,
even when the library's own default disagrees. Where he gives none — the
TR-808's six cymbal frequencies, the claves' pitch, the flute's high-pass
corner, the Leslie's rotation speeds — the lesson says the number is the
patch's choice rather than passing it off as his.

And some of these recipes do not work, which Reid says himself at the end of
the piano articles. Knowing which corner of an instrument a subtractive
synthesiser cannot turn is worth as much as any of the patches.
