The most useful thing in Reid's two articles on the Leslie cabinet is that it
is not one effect. A horn swinging round on the end of a duct does three
separate things at once, and the phase between them is the whole character.

It is loudest when it points at you, and moving fastest towards you a quarter
of a turn earlier — so the vibrato is ninety degrees out of phase with the
tremolo. And it is duller when it points away, which is the same moment as
being quieter, so the tone modulation is **in phase** with the loudness
modulation rather than ninety degrees from it.

<Patch id="recipes/leslie" show={["speed", "depth", "crossover"]} />

<Try>

Press Play. Nothing is gated here: the cabinet is the lesson, so the organ
underneath it just drones. Switch **Speed** to **Tremolo** and listen to the
spin-up rather than to the destination. The horn arrives before the drum,
because they have different transition rates as well as different speeds.

</Try>

<Try>

Take **Depth** to zero and back up. The loudness and the tone move together and
neither of them is the pitch — the pitch wobble stays at one percent whatever
you do here, and that is deliberate.

</Try>

<Try>

Move **Crossover**. Eight hundred hertz is Reid's number: above it goes to the
horn and below it to the drum, and dragging the corner up hands the whole sound
to the slow rotor.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

The Doppler shift in a real cabinet is small — Reid measures it at about plus
or minus one percent — and that fixes the delay depth rather than leaving it to
taste. A sinusoidal delay of amplitude A at rate r shifts the pitch by two pi r
A, so the depth here is one percent divided by two pi times the rate: about two
milliseconds slow, a third of that fast. Change speed and the depth follows, so
the wobble stays at one percent.

</Aside>

<Aside kind="note">

Reid gives no rotation speeds at all, in hertz or in revolutions per minute.
The only figure in the two articles is that the rotor can be slower than one
hertz. So the 0.9 and 6.5 hertz here, and the drum running at eight tenths of
the horn's rate, are this patch's choices — and the spin-up is his, a slew
generator smoothing between two speed control voltages.

</Aside>
