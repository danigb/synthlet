A Hammond's nine drawbars are harmonics 1, 3, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16 of the
sixteen foot pitch. Read that list again: there is no fifth harmonic on a
Hammond, and no seventh, ninth, eleventh, thirteenth, fourteenth or fifteenth
either. Every registration ever played is built from those nine, at nine
positions each, which is where Reid's figure of 387,420,489 registrations per
manual comes from.

The key sounds the eight foot, so relative to the note you play the ratios are
a half, one and a half, one, two, three, four, five, six and eight. The
drawbars are labelled by footage rather than by harmonic, which is an organ
builder's habit borrowed from pipe lengths, and it is why the first two are out
of numerical order.

<Try>

Hold a note and move the drawbars while it sounds. Nothing fades in and nothing
fades out: a Hammond key is a switch that connects nine tonewheels to a bus, so
there is no attack, no decay and no release anywhere in this patch.

</Try>

<Try>

Press the four registration buttons in turn. They are all Reid's: **88 8000
000** is the one Jimmy Smith and Keith Emerson lived on, **88 8888 888** is
everything, **83 4211 100** is as close as nine drawbars with seven harmonics
missing get to a sawtooth, and **00 8030 200** is as close as they get to odd
harmonics only.

</Try>

<Try>

Set **Percussion** to **Second** and play a staccato phrase, then hold one note
down and play another. The second one has no accent. Percussion on a real
Hammond is single-triggering, and it also drops the level of the sustained part
of the sound — both of those are here, and both are Reid's.

</Try>

<Try>

Turn **Key click** off and on. On the instrument it is nine contacts closing at
nine slightly different moments, and it was considered a fault for about twenty
years before it was considered the sound.

</Try>
