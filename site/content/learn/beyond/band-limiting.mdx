Part 17 states the sampling theorem and then does the thing that makes it
audible. Sample a 10 kHz sine at 13.33 kHz and the samples still lie on the
original wave, but they also describe a different one at 3.33 kHz. Anything
above half the sample rate folds over and reappears the same distance below it,
under an alias — and once it is there, Reid says, you cannot take it out again.

A sawtooth at 4 kHz has harmonics at 8, 12 and 16 kHz and onwards, so most of
them are over that line before the note is even played. What an oscillator does
about that is the whole of this page.

<Try>

Press Play on **Naive wavetable**. The marks are where the harmonics of 4 kHz
ought to be; the trace has lines standing everywhere between them. Those are
harmonics that went over Nyquist and came back down. Now sweep **Frequency**
upward and watch them: the marks climb, and the aliases slide the other way,
which is the one behaviour no real harmonic has.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch to **PolyBLEP** at the same pitch. The space between the marks empties.
The library's oscillator replaces each jump in the waveform with a band-limited
step, so the offending harmonics are never generated rather than removed
afterwards — which matters, because removing them afterwards is impossible.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch to the decimated arm and drag **Sample rate** down from 44100. This is
the clean saw resampled too slowly with the anti-alias filter switched off, so
the spray comes back on demand. Turn **Anti-alias** on and it cleans up: that
switch is the low-pass filter Reid puts in front of every converter. **Bits** is
his other limit, how finely the amplitude can be chopped up.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

The naive arm is a plain buffer read at whatever rate the pitch needs, and it is
not a module in this library on purpose. The library's job is not to ship a
broken oscillator; this lesson's job is to show one. Everything that is wrong
with it comes from one place — the discontinuity where the ramp wraps, resampled
by straight-line interpolation.

</Aside>
