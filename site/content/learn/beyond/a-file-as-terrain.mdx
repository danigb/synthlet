The previous page granulated a stream. Grains were taken out of the last few
seconds of whatever was arriving, which is a granular _delay_: any source, one
window of time, and a position measured backwards from now.

A granular _sampler_ is the other arrangement, and it is a different instrument
rather than a better one. It holds a whole decoded file and keeps an absolute
playhead somewhere inside it. Grains are taken from wherever that playhead is,
and the playhead moves at a speed you set — the scan rate. Because the grains
carry the pitch and the scanner only decides where they are read from, that
speed has nothing to do with the pitch at all.

Once the scan rate is a knob, the interesting settings are the ones a tape
machine cannot reach. At one, the playhead keeps up with the grains and you hear
the file at its own speed. At a tenth, you crawl: the same material, an octave
of detail stretched out, still at the original pitch. At zero the playhead stops
and the grains keep coming, so one instant of the file becomes a held chord —
the sound of a freeze, except that you chose the moment rather than catching it.
Negative and the whole thing runs backwards, still in tune.

That is why the page calls a file terrain. A sample is usually a thing you
trigger; here it is a landscape with a position in it, and playing the instrument
is moving around.

There is no widget yet. The module this lesson needs is Strata, the library's
granular sampler, and specifically its scanner; the package is designed and not
written. The granular delay from the previous page cannot stand in for it,
because its position is a fraction of a rolling few seconds of input and it has
no scan rate at all — which is exactly the difference this page is about.
