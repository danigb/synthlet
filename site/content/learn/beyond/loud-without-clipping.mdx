There is nothing after your output. A browser hands the signal to a converter
with no headroom past full scale, no console to ride and nobody in the control
room, so anything that goes over the top is simply chopped off — and four voices
summed will find the top on their own. A limiter is the answer, and it is the
one module in this library that belongs in every patch whether or not it is part
of the sound.

<Patch id="beyond/limiter" show={["threshold", "gain", "release", "bypass"]} />

<Try>

Press Play and watch the two meters. They agree, because nothing is over the
threshold yet. Now push **Gain** up a few decibels at a time. The Before meter
climbs steadily; the After meter climbs and then stops, pinned just under the
ceiling, however much more you give it.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch **Bypass** on at a high gain. Same signal, same delay, no limiting — and
the After meter shoots past zero, which is where the chopping happens. Switch it
back and forth a few times: the loudness barely changes, and the damage does.

</Try>

<Try>

Set a short **Release** and a threshold around −6, then a long one. Short and it
recovers between every note, which is audible as a pumping; long and it stays
turned down through the gaps. Neither is wrong. The knob is how quickly the
limiter is willing to forget a peak.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

A limiter this clean is only possible because it is allowed to be late. It
delays the signal by a couple of milliseconds so that it can see a peak coming
and already be turned down by the time it arrives, rather than reacting after
the fact. That is why the bypass path here is delayed by exactly the same
amount: without it the comparison would be a comb filter as well as a bypass,
and neither arm would be telling the truth about the other.

</Aside>

<Aside kind="note">

The threshold is written in decibels true peak, which measures the waveform
_between_ the samples as well as on them — a signal can sit under the ceiling at
every sample and still go over it on the way past. The meters here can show peak
and loudness and cannot yet show true peak, so the number on the knob is a
promise the page cannot quite check.

</Aside>
