---
title: "Beyond the book"
description: "The shelf this library ships that Synth Secrets had no chapter for, because in 2004 most of it was still a research topic."
---

Synth Secrets ran from May 1999 to July 2004, sixty-three monthly instalments,
and it is an analogue series. Its subject is voltage: oscillators that are
circuits, filters that are ladders of transistors, and the handful of digital
effects that had become cheap enough to bolt onto the end. Where it does discuss
digital audio it does so from first principles, in terms of sample-and-hold
circuits and flip-flops, because that was the honest way to explain it to the
people reading.

A great deal happened outside that frame, and some of it happened while the
series was still running. Wavetables existed — the PPG Wave was 1981 — but they
were a niche until software synths made a stack of waveforms cheap. Granular
synthesis was an idea from 1947 and a technique academic studios used; it became
something you could put on a page about twenty years after Reid stopped writing.
Time-stretching that leaves the pitch alone was a studio service, not a knob.
The loudness unit that broadcasters now measure in was standardised in 2011,
seven years too late for the series.

This chapter is what the library ships that Reid had no chapter for. It opens
with the one thing that makes digital oscillators sound wrong when they sound
wrong — aliasing, which Part 17 does explain and which nothing in the 1970s had
to worry about — and then works through the shelf: a table of waves and a knob
that slides between them, sound cut into grains, time and pitch pulled apart,
imperfection offered as a parameter, and the limiter that belongs on the last
node of anything that plays in a browser.

Two of the eight pages are written and not yet playable. They say so, and they
say what they are waiting for.
