Every delay in the previous chapter was a strip of tape, and the analogy held
right up to its last page: move the heads while the tape is running and the
pitch bends. That is not a quirk of the model, it is the arithmetic. On tape,
speed and pitch are one quantity, because the samples of the sound arrive closer
together or further apart and that spacing is both the duration and the
frequency.

This module cuts the tie.

<Try>

Press Play. The clip is already running at half speed. Drag **Speed** from 0.25
up to 4 and back: the whole thing takes four times as long or a quarter as long,
and the voices and instruments in it stay exactly where they were on the
keyboard. Compare that with the previous chapter's tape delay, where moving the
time knob bent everything.

</Try>

<Try>

Put **Speed** back to 1 and take **Detune** to 700 cents. Now the pitch has gone
up a fifth and the clip still takes precisely as long as it did. The two sliders
do not know about each other, so any combination of the two is available —
double speed a fourth down, quarter speed an octave up.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch **Reverse** on at a slow speed. Backwards, stretched, in tune. **Loop** is
on by default so the clip keeps going; switch it off and it plays once and stops.

</Try>

<Aside kind="why">

The technique is called WSOLA, and the idea is simple enough to state. Chop the
sound into overlapping frames. To stretch time, repeat some frames; to compress
it, skip some. The catch is that a repeated frame almost never lines up with
what came before it, and a join that does not line up is a click — so before
overlapping two frames, slide one of them a little either way and keep the
position where the two waveforms best resemble each other. The samples still
come out at the rate they went in, which is why the pitch does not move.

</Aside>
