Every oscillator so far has had one waveform at a time, chosen from a list. A
wavetable oscillator has a pile of them — planes — and a read position that can
sit between two planes and crossfade. The waveform stops being a setting and
becomes a place you are, which means it can be swept, envelope-controlled or
modulated like any other parameter.

<Patch id="beyond/wavetable" show={["table", "morph", "lfoRate", "lfoDepth"]} />

<Try>

Press Play and drag **Morph** slowly from 0 to 1 on the built-in table. Sine,
triangle, sawtooth, square — the four waveforms of chapter 1, in order, with
everything in between. Watch the partials on the spectrum arrive and leave as
you cross each plane: they arrive standing on the marks, which is what makes
this a spectral interpolation rather than two waves fighting.

</Try>

<Try>

Switch **Table** to the harmonic sweep. Each plane is a sawtooth truncated one
harmonic further, so morphing is additive synthesis done with a single knob:
plane one is a sine, plane sixteen has sixteen partials. Then try the odd
harmonics table, which is the same walk from a sine but arrives at a square
instead.

</Try>

<Try>

Leave **Morph** at about 0.3, put **LFO depth** at 0.2 and **LFO rate** at half
a hertz. The knob and the oscillator sum at the same parameter, so the LFO
wanders around wherever you left the knob rather than replacing it. That is a
morph knob with a modulation amount beside it, and it is most of what makes a
wavetable patch sound alive.

</Try>

<Aside kind="note">

The three tables here are built in the patch, out of arrays of harmonic
magnitudes, one array per plane. That is deliberate: the module can also fetch
tables from an online catalogue, and a lesson that depends on somebody else's
web host is a lesson that breaks when they reorganise it. The first array is not
the DC offset, as it would be in Web Audio's own `PeriodicWave` — it is the
fundamental.

</Aside>
