Moving the delay time on a tape or bucket-brigade machine **bends the pitch of
everything already in the line**. The Space Echo's Repeat Rate knob is a
performance control precisely because of that, and the Echoplex's sliding head
is the same effect made mechanical. `AnalogDelay`'s read head travels to its new
position rather than handing over to a second one, so it resamples the buffer on
the way.

There is a second difference that matters more for the model's honesty. In a
clean digital delay, `time` and `tone` are independent. **In real analog
hardware they are not.** A bucket-brigade's delay is its stage count over its
clock rate, so lengthening the delay lowers the clock, which drags the
anti-alias and reconstruction filters down with it, which darkens every repeat.
Tape does the same through head gap and transport speed. So there is **no
`tone` parameter**: bandwidth is derived from `time` and `age`.

```ts
import { AnalogDelay, AnalogDelayMode } from "synthlet";

const delay = AnalogDelay(audioContext, {
  time: 0.3, // seconds
  feedback: 0.6,
  mix: 0.4,
  taps: 0.7, // more heads open
  age: 0.4, // wobblier, dirtier, darker, hissier
  mode: AnalogDelayMode.Tape,
});

source.connect(delay).connect(audioContext.destination);
```

## Parameters

- `time`: record head to first playback head, in seconds [0.02, 1.5]. **Moving
  it bends pitch.** The range is narrower than `DigitalDelay`'s because the
  hardware is: an MN3005 tops out at 204.8 ms and an EP-3 at roughly 800 ms
- `feedback`: loop gain [0, 1.2] — the Space Echo's _Intensity_. Above 1
  self-oscillates, bounded by the loop's soft limiter
- `mix`: dry/wet [0, 1]
- `taps`: level envelope across the mode's tap positions [0, 1]. 0 is the first
  head only; later heads fade in as it rises
- `age`: one composite wear control [0, 1] — more wobble, more saturation, less
  bandwidth, more hiss, all on one curve
- `wobble`: wow and flutter depth [0, 1], scaling on top of whatever `age`
  implies. A well-maintained machine with an eccentric capstan is a real
  combination that one knob cannot express
- `spread`: L/R time offset as a ratio [0, 1] — two machines, lightly apart. 0
  is mono-compatible
- `mode`: `AnalogDelayMode`, 0 Tape and 1 BBD. Intermediate values wipe between
  them rather than snapping

One construction option, `maxTime` (default 1.5 s), sizes the buffers.

## Why the mode enum is worth its slot

The two modes read the **same** delay line at different tap positions. Tape's
are integer multiples of `time` — 1 : 2 : 3, which Roland's own RE-202 manual
confirms for the RE-201's heads, and 1 : 2 : 3 : 4 for the Echorec's drum by
geometry — so opening more heads gives **rhythmic subdivisions**. The MN3011's
six taps sit at stages 396 / 662 / 1194 / 1726 / 2790 / 3328, giving ratios of
1 : 1.67 : 3.01 : 4.36 : 7.05 : 8.40 — deliberately irrational, and its
datasheet says why: mixing them yields a "natural reverberation effect". So the
same `taps` control gives a **diffuse wash** there. Both are physically true,
and the difference is measured rather than asserted: the envelope's
autocorrelation at the tap period reads 0.78 on Tape and 0.02 on BBD.

## What it deliberately isn't

There is no `tone`, no `cross`, no tempo sync, no pitch shift, no freeze and no
reverse. Tape hysteresis, head bump, print-through and dropout are all real and
all modelled somewhere in the literature, and none of them are here: `age` as a
single composite curve is the mechanism that keeps the scope bounded.

The other half of the delay space — precise, clean, pitch-preserving — is
[`DigitalDelay`](/docs/digital-delay). One question tells you which you want:
character and drift, or precision?

## Sourced numbers

The provenance here is datasheets and service manuals rather than papers, and
some of it is not sourced at all. The **wow and flutter depths, the flutter
rate, the tape gap-loss constant, the `age` curve and the compander's constants
are chosen by ear**, and labelled as chosen in the source. No sourced
wow/flutter figure exists for the RE-201, EP-3 or Echorec; studio-deck standards
(DIN 0.2%) are explicitly not applicable, because tape echoes are widely
described as far worse and nobody quantifies it. The one derived figure is the
**1.2 Hz wow rate**, which is the Echorec's 71 RPM drum: wow is
once-per-revolution eccentricity, so the rotation rate is the wow rate.
