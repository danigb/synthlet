`@synthlet/modal-resonator`

Every percussion chapter of Synth Secrets ends with a table: a ratio, a level
and a decay for each partial of the instrument, followed by a struggle to play
it with four oscillators and four contour generators. The kettle drum needs a
DX7; the bell needs a Nord Modular's sine bank. This module plays the table.
Strike it and every mode rings at once.

```ts
import {
  registerModalResonatorWorklet,
  Impulse,
  ModalResonator,
} from "synthlet";

const audioContext = new AudioContext();
await registerModalResonatorWorklet(audioContext);

const strike = Impulse(audioContext, { trigger });
const timp = ModalResonator(audioContext, {
  frequency: 150,
  decay: 2,
  modes: ModalResonator.modes.kettleDrum(),
});
strike.connect(timp).connect(audioContext.destination);
```

## A kettle drum is four numbers per mode

Part 32 measures the kettle drum and hands over this — the four radial modes a
timpanist favours, with the book's own amplitudes and decays:

| Mode | Ratio | At 150 Hz | Level | Decay |
| ---- | ----- | --------- | ----- | ----- |
| 1,1  | 1.00  | 150 Hz    | 5     | 45 %  |
| 2,1  | 1.50  | 225 Hz    | 4     | 73 %  |
| 3,1  | 1.98  | 297 Hz    | 3     | 91 %  |
| 4,1  | 2.44  | 366 Hz    | 1     | 84 %  |

That is `ModalResonator.modes.kettleDrum()`, verbatim. Reid's reason for
favouring those modes is a sentence about playing technique: the timpanist
strikes _"almost precisely a quarter the way from the edge to the centre"_,
which suppresses the enharmonic circular modes. Pick **Membrane** in the example
to hear what striking the centre gives you — Part 31's twelve Bessel ratios,
before the drum's air drags the radial ones towards harmonic.

Watch the spectrum while you do it. A mode's peak there is proportional to its
level _times_ its decay, so the drum's tallest line is mode 3, not the mode 1
that is loudest at the strike. The spectrum and the strike are two different
questions.

## Parameters

| Param        | Default | Range     | Rate   | Meaning                                                        |
| ------------ | ------- | --------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `frequency`  | 220     | 20 … 5000 | k-rate | Frequency of a ratio-1 mode in Hz; every mode is a ratio of it |
| `decay`      | 1       | 0.01 … 10 | k-rate | Seconds for a mode whose table decay is 1 to fall 60 dB        |
| `brightness` | 1       | 0 … 1     | k-rate | Mode n's level is scaled by `brightness^(n−1)`; 1 is the table |

`frequency` and `decay` are read once per block and interpolated across it, so
an envelope on `frequency` — every drum has one — neither zippers nor changes
the loudness of the ring. `brightness` is the strike-hardness gesture of Parts
35 and 42: a harder hit excites more of the upper modes, so velocity into it is
how hard the stick lands.

`decay` is the time of a mode whose _table_ decay is 1, not the time of mode 1:
the kettle drum's mode 1 is the 45 %, and it goes in as written.

Construction options: `maxModes` (default 32, at most 256) sizes the pool so a
table swap never allocates, and `modes` is the starting table (default
`harmonic(8)`).

## The tables

`node.setModes(table)` replaces the table on the next render quantum. Modes in
both tables keep ringing; a table longer than `maxModes` is truncated with a
warning.

| Table               | From    | What it is                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `harmonic(n)`       | —       | Ratios 1 … n, each at `1/n`. `harmonic(1)` is claves: one mode               |
| `membrane()`        | Part 31 | The twelve Bessel ratios of an ideal membrane                                |
| `kettleDrum()`      | Part 32 | The table above                                                              |
| `bell()`            | Part 40 | Strike partials 2 : 3 : 4, so you hear the missing 1; a beating pair; a hum  |
| `cowbell()`         | Part 41 | 587 and 845 Hz at `frequency: 587`, each as a short impact and a longer tail |
| `stiffString(B, n)` | —       | `k·√(1 + B·k²)`, the stretched partials of a stiff string                    |

The bell is the book's best trick: its three loudest partials sit at 2, 3 and 4
times a fundamental that is not there, and the ear supplies it — so
`frequency` is the pitch you hear, not a frequency present in the sound. The
near-degenerate pair at 2 and 2.01 is the _"boii-yoy-yoy-yoiinnnggg"_. Only the
ratios are Reid's; the levels and decays are this library's.

A table is a plain array, so a rule is a function: `stiffString` is the first,
and anything that returns `{ ratio, level, decay }[]` is the next.

## Driving it continuously

Levels are normalised for a **strike**: a unit impulse puts each mode's envelope
at its level. Anything longer is amplified by the resonance, and a long decay is
a very narrow one — a unit sine held on a mode with `decay: 1` peaks at about
3200 (+70 dB), and a 30 ms noise burst into `harmonic(8)` peaks at 2.57. Put a
`Gain` in front of any exciter that is not an `Impulse`. The example's noise
burst goes in at 0.05.

## Mono, deliberately

Input channels are summed and the output is one channel. A body is one object,
and sixty-four resonators per channel would double the cost for a stereo image
that a `StereoPannerNode` after the node gives for free.

## Not here

No `structure` or `position` knob yet — they are Rings' ways of filling the
table without a table, and the table has to exist first. No exciter: `Impulse`,
`Noise`, `AdAmp` and every oscillator already are one. And no chaos: Part 37's
hard-hit cymbal couples its modes nonlinearly, which a linear bank cannot do;
`Noise` under a velocity-scaled envelope is the book's own workaround.
